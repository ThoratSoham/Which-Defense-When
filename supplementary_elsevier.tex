\section{Detailed 12-agent broadcast-tree calibration and partial protection tables}
\label{supp:net12}

% [KL-059] [KL-060]
Tables~\ref{tab:supp_net12a} and~\ref{tab:supp_net12b} present full cell-level measurements for the 12-agent broadcast-tree probe (\NEpisodesTwelveAgent\ episodes = \NCallsTwelveAgent\ LLM calls). 

Three structural considerations explain why this probe is treated as suggestive:
(1)~On the 8-episode chain calibration cohort, the Ensemble estimated downstream relay at $r = \RhoRepass$, collapsing expected infection size on any 12-node spanning tree to $1 + 11o$ ($\ExpectedTreeInfectionOQuarter$ for $o=\OQuarter$, $\ExpectedTreeInfectionOEighth$ for $o=\OEighth$), producing identical predictions across all three topologies;
(2)~At $n=8$ episodes/cell, empirical 95\% CIs are $\CIRangeWide$ agents wide; and
(3)~In the 12-node BFS broadcast tree of \texttt{ba12}, only 3 nodes (0, 1, 5) have outgoing edges, which coincide exactly with the top-3 highest-degree hubs, making hub-first protection invariant for $p \ge \BFSPActiveThreshold$.

\section{Exploratory robustness probes: threshold sweeps, encodings, and adversarial compliance}
\label{supp:probes}

% [KL-052] [KL-053] [KL-054]
\paragraph{Twelve-secret threshold sweep}
Across 12 diverse semantic secrets (Figure~\ref{fig:supp_other}), the deployed threshold of \ThreshCosine\ achieved \ParaphraseRecallGateway\ paraphrase recall with \OverblockHardNegRange\ false positives on same-topic hard negatives. Requiring three shared words in the lexical rule yielded \ProbeLexicalThreeWordRecall\ recall and \ProbeLexicalThreeWordFP\ false positives at \ThreshCosine, confirming that dense embedding similarity drives hard-negative false positives. Spanish translations were missed at all thresholds $\ge \ProbeSpanishCutoffThresh$.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\figbox{fig6-other-secrets.png}{0.65}
\caption{Exploratory 12-secret threshold sweep: paraphrase recall vs.\ hard-negative false-positive rate across cosine thresholds.}
\label{fig:supp_other}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Encoding and translation probe (12 trials)}
In scripted detector tests on the exploratory renal-failure secret (20 trials/format at threshold \ProbeSpanishCutoffThresh), L1+L2 caught exact, unhyphenated, and dashed IDs (\ProbeScriptedRewordedRecall) as well as reworded (\ProbeScriptedRewordedRecall), abbreviated (\ProbeScriptedAbbrevRecall), and vague (\ProbeScriptedVagueRecall) paraphrases, but missed base64, character-reversed, and foreign-translated leaks (recall $\approx 0$). An exploratory patch tuned on that secret restored encoded-ID recall to \ProbeScriptedRewordedRecall\ and raised translation recall from \ProbeTransPreRecall\ to \ProbeTransPostRecall, but reduced English paraphrase recall from \ProbeSpanishCutoffThresh\ to \ProbeEnglishPostRecall.

\paragraph{Adversarial compliance probe on 2B--3B models}
When Agent~0 in a 3-agent chain was instructed to exfiltrate secrets in disguise (30 episodes/cell), \texttt{llama3.2:3b} ($\AdvInfectLlama$ infected) and \texttt{qwen2.5:3b} ($\AdvInfectQwenVerbatim$ verbatim, $\AdvInfectQwenSemantic$ semantic) largely refused or omitted the secret, and \texttt{gemma2:2b} complied (\AdversarialInfectedVerbatim\ verbatim, \AdversarialInfectedSemantic\ semantic infected) but wrote the secret in plain English in every episode ($0$ encodings). Consequently, the gateway blocked all ID leaks and \AdversarialBlockedEnglish\ plain-English leaks ($\AdvBlockedLlama$ and $\AdvBlockedGemma$ infected). Capable adversarial evaluations require larger models or scripted injections.
